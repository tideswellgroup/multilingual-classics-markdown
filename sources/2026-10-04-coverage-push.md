# Source qualification: the 2026-10-04 coverage push

Twelve locales on the wishlist were investigated for one book each: mr, gu, pa, or, te, kn, ml, si, lo, km, my and bo. Eight were added; four stay open. This file is the qualification record for every candidate that was seriously considered, so that a later contributor can see why each pick was made and why each rejection stands, and need not repeat the search.

Method. Each candidate went through the triage in order, cheapest disqualifier first: can the work be shipped (published before 1929, or a manuscript work whose author died before 1855; secular; no translation under copyright), can the text be got, is it usable (real Unicode text in its own script, not a legacy font encoding or uncorrected OCR), and does it mean what it appears to (a named edition, or for a modern work a clean typed transcription). Upstream servers were queried through their APIs at about one request a second with a User-Agent carrying a contact address, and Retry-After was honoured. Licence terms are quoted verbatim; anything labelled interpretation is a judgment call, not a legal finding. Last verified for every entry: 2026-10-04.

Encoding was checked on every adopted text by decoding the whole file and measuring the share of letters in the script's own Unicode block, with a sampled word's code points recorded in the book's source_note. The percentage alone is not enough: a Sinhala novel on Wikibooks scores 99.6 percent while being machine-converted from a legacy font and corrupt (rakaransaya written without the zero-width joiner 1,238 times against 25 correct), so conjunct and ordering checks were run as well, and a Burmese check for Zawgyi ordering.

Licences, verbatim. Every Wikisource site reports its licence through the MediaWiki siteinfo API as "Creative Commons Attribution-Share Alike 4.0"; that covers contributors' edits, and the corpus treats faithful transcriptions of public-domain texts as public domain, as it does for its other Wikisource books. Page and scan templates: te `PD-old` (page) and `PD-old-auto-1923|deathyear=1915` (Commons); kn `PD-scan|PD-old-auto|deathyear=1937` (Commons); mr `PD-US-expired` and `PD-India` (Commons); gu `પ્રકાશન-ભારત` (page) and `PD-1923` (Commons); or `cc-zero` (Commons, the digitisers' dedication); pa `PD-scan` (Commons); ml none. OTDO, from `https://otdo.aa-ken.jp/about`: "The materials published on this site is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License (CC-BY)."

Interpretation, bo. OTDO's transcription is a diplomatic reading by named scholars and carries an editorial layer the corpus does not own. CC BY 4.0 permits redistribution with attribution, which the book's source_note carries, and the README names the book alongside the other one with such a layer. This was put to the maintainer before acquisition and accepted.

Change signal. The Wikisource pages can be edited at any time, so each book records its retrieval date; OTDO revises its texts in batches (2018 to 2024 so far), so the bo book records the SHA-256 of the page as retrieved. A re-run of the converter against the live page is the freshness check for each.

Picks passed over, with reasons:

- **mr**: T. N. Atre, *Gaon-Gada* (1915), more canonical, but six pages are still uncorrected OCR; the candidate for a second book. Jotiba Phule's *Gulamgiri* is not on mr.wikisource, and *Shetkaryacha Asud* has no scan or named edition. Apte's novels and Keshavsut's poems have no scan-backed transcription.
- **gu**: Nandshankar Mehta, *Karan Ghelo* (1926 ninth edition, validated) is labelled a school edition and may be abridged. Dalpatram's *Mithyabhiman* lost its scan from Commons in July 2025 as a modern edited edition.
- **pa**: Waris Shah, *Heer*: the transcription's edition is undated (the 1900 date is an uploader's claim) and its title page advertises additions, meaning an interpolated text. Qadir Yar's *Sohni Mahiwal* (1912) and Mitt Singh's *Kissa Raja Rasalu* (1914) are clean but less canonical. *Punjabi Baat Cheet* (1884) has pages missing.
- **or**: *Galpa Swalpa* and *Sashi Sena* were weaker on edition evidence; no transcription of a pre-1929 printing of *Chha Mana Atha Guntha* exists (Internet Archive holds a 1964 twelfth edition).
- **te**: Veeresalingam's *Rajasekhara Charitramu* is transcribed from a 1987 edited reprint. The first-edition text of *Kanyasulkamu* is transcribed only from a 2012 edited collected works. Vemana and Sumati verse pages interleave modern glosses and name no edition.
- **kn**: M. S. Puttanna's *Madiddunno Maharaya* (1915) is unproofread OCR; Nanjangud Tirumalamba's *Sushile* (1922) is a strong runner-up, validated, less canonical; Kempu Narayana and Gulvadi Venkata Rao are not on kn.wikisource; the Sarvajna page is an unsourced paste with heavy devotional content.
- **ml**: Appu Nedungadi's *Kundalatha* (1887) is scan-backed but the scan is a mid-twentieth-century reprint; Chandu Menon's *Sarada* is unfinished.
- **bo**: Sakya Pandita's *Treasury of Aphoristic Jewels* exists only as a woodblock scan whose OCR is unusable; the songs of Tsangyang Gyatso and the Gesar epic exist in Unicode only in modern editions with their own editorial layers.

## Left open

**si (Sinhala). Defer.** si.wikisource does not exist; the Sinhala collection on the multilingual wikisource.org is mostly Buddhist canon and Jataka retellings (excluded), skeletons without text, a Rajavaliya carrying modern critical apparatus, and works of unknown rights. Piyadasa Sirisena's *Jayatissa saha Rosalin* on Sinhala Wikibooks follows a modern collected edition and its Unicode is corrupt (see Method). A. Simon de Silva's *Meena* (1905) on Internet Archive is raw OCR of an unverified edition. The one text that passes the hard rules on paper, M. L. Silva's *Sirit Maldama* (children's conduct verse, about 17,000 characters, dated 1895 only by a wiki header), is not canonical and its date is unproven. Paths: a proofread transcription of *Meena* or *Jayatissa saha Rosalin* from a dated pre-1929 scan; or a classical secular sandesa poem from a named nineteenth-century printing.

**my (Burmese). Defer.** All candidates checked were real Unicode, with no Zawgyi ordering. U Ya's *Twelve-Season Luta* (composed 1781; printed by the Hanthawaddy Press, Rangoon, 1907) is on my.wikisource as fourteen pages whose base text follows a named manuscript, with variant readings from three other witnesses, the 1907 print among them, in footnotes. A collation of four witnesses entered by a wiki contributor in 2018 is almost certainly typed from a modern critical edition whose editor is not named, and that is exactly the case the corpus's named-edition rule exists to refuse; the cycle's prelude is also substantially Buddhist praise. *Mwe Nun Yagan* and Shin Htwe Nyo's *Pyay Sun Mawgun* name no edition at all. No digital text of James Hla Kyaw's *Maung Yin Maung Ma Me Ma* (1904) or U Latt's *Sabebin* (1914) was found. Path: a transcription of the 1907 Hanthawaddy printing of the Luta, if a scan can be found.

**km (Khmer). Reject for now.** Krom Ngoy's chbab (dated 1922 inside the poems) were first published by the Buddhist Institute after its founding in 1930, and the author died in 1936, so neither the publication rule nor the manuscript exception applies. Ukña Suttantaprija Ind's *Gatilok* is a Buddhist conduct manual with no proven pre-1929 printing. *Prachum Roeung Preng Khmer* was first printed in the 1960s. The 1927 issues of *Kambuja Suriya* were published in time but survive as scans only, and their content is mostly religious. Tum Teav has no transcription with pre-1929 print evidence.

**lo (Lao). Reject.** lo.wikisource does not exist and the multilingual Wikisource holds only modern Lao law. Internet Archive's few pre-1929 Lao items are a Psalter, a lexicon, and page images of a modern compilation. The Digital Library of Lao Manuscripts holds images and catalogue data, no transcriptions. Lao literary printing largely begins in the 1930s; the classics survive in palm-leaf manuscripts and post-1940 editions. A future route is an openly licensed typed transcription of a named palm-leaf manuscript, under the manuscript exception as for bo.
