A full-corpus review of all 134 books (the chr Constitution excluded: it is frozen for hand maintenance with its own worklist in QUALITY.md). Two lenses per book: a literary/language reading for content integrity (garbles, truncation, wrong language, page furniture, broken chapter sequences), and an engineering reading for markdown validity and frontmatter usefulness. Method: a scripted mechanical pass over every file (frontmatter validity, required keys, code fences, heading-level jumps, control characters, mojibake, table shape), then seventeen parallel machine reading passes, one per locale group, each sampling openings, endings, interior windows, and chapter seams per the QUALITY.md audit method, with full reads for short books. Those reading passes were made by a language model, not by seventeen people. They are good at what this pass was looking for, which is structural and mechanical damage that survives conversion, and they are no substitute for the native-speaker reads that QUALITY.md still lists as outstanding for several books. Findings were treated as leads until checked against the file, which is why one of them is recorded below as refuted.

Severities: **HIGH** = text integrity broken (missing, duplicated, or wrong text). **MEDIUM** = a defect a reader or downstream consumer trips on. **LOW** = minor blemish. **COSMETIC** = trivial. Findings marked *(verified)* were independently re-checked against the file after the reviewer reported them; the three HIGH findings were all verified, and one reviewer claim was refuted and is recorded as such.

## Summary

The corpus is in good shape. 87 of 134 books came back with no findings at all, and the mechanical layer is clean: all 135 frontmatter blocks parse and carry the required provenance keys, with no unclosed fences, no heading-level jumps, and no encoding damage. The three text-integrity breaks below are the items worth fixing first; the MEDIUM list is dominated by two recurring, systematic patterns rather than scattered damage: (1) chapter or section titles left as plain text (or wrongly promoted to headings) by the various converters, and (2) frontmatter notes whose claims have drifted from what the body actually contains.
